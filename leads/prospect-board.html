Who you are actually selling to

Owner-operated vacation-rental and villa management companies, 15–150 properties, in visually-driven leisure destinations — where the founder still personally approves marketing spend.

Why this profile holds together

They own your raw material

Every managed villa already has a professional photo set. You need no new assets — the job starts the moment they say yes.

The pain repeats

Every property they onboard needs marketing assets. That turns a one-time sale into a per-property retainer.

The owner is the buyer

At this size there is no marketing department. The founder reads the WhatsApp and decides the same day.

$150 is under the thinking threshold

For a firm billing 20–30% on dozens of properties, this is a line item, not a capital decision.

They already believe in video

They are measured on booking conversion. You are not educating them on why video matters — only on why yours is cheap.

They are reachable

Every contact here is a business line the company publishes itself, for the express purpose of being contacted.

What to actually send

WhatsApp — Bali, Thailand, Dubai

Hi [Name] — I saw [Villa Name] on your site, beautiful property.

I turn villa photos into 30-second walkthrough videos for Airbnb and Instagram. No filming, no visit — I just use the photos you already have. Around $150 per villa.

I made one from your listing photos so you can judge it. Want me to send it?

Under 60 words, and no attachment on message one — attachments trigger blocks. Asking permission to send converts better than dumping a link, and it will not get you reported.

Email — US, Europe, Australia

Subject: [Villa Name] — 30-second walkthrough, no filming needed

Hi [Name],

I made a short walkthrough video from the photos already on your [Villa Name] listing — no shoot, no site visit. It is 28 seconds: [link]

If it is useful, I can do the rest of your portfolio at $150 per property.

If not, tell me and I will not follow up.

[Your name] — [company], [city]
Reply STOP to opt out.

Those last two lines are not optional in the EU, UK or Australia. They also lift reply rates, because a visible exit signals a real business.

The part that matters more than the list

Build the sample before you send. You have Runway and Kling, and their photos are public on their own websites. A 30-second video built from their villa converts at a completely different rate than any description of what you could do. Do this for the first ten leads before you touch the other forty. At $150 a unit your bottleneck is not lead volume — it is proof.

Before your first send

Every contact here is a business address the company publishes on its own site. No personal data of private individuals was collected. But the details were read from search results rather than the live pages, so treat this as a verified-target list with unverified contact strings.

  • Open each site and confirm the email or phone still matches. The status line on each card tells you what was published.
  • For cards marked site only, pull the address from their contact page — about twenty of them.
  • Find the founder's name on the About page or LinkedIn. A named greeting roughly doubles reply rates over "Hi there".
  • Run emails through a verifier before any bulk send. One bounce-heavy send can permanently damage your sending domain.
  • Send from a separate domain to your main one. Warm it two to three weeks, then cap at 30–40 per inbox per day.
Roughly ninety minutes of verification for all fifty. Do not skip it — it is the difference between a working list and a burned domain.
